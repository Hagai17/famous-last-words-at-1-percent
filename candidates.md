# Candidates — Famous Last Words at 1 Percent

Task: the funniest one line of last words a phone says as its battery hits
1 percent. Gate: a single line, 1–199 characters, written to
`/output/entry.txt`.

Judge brief (from the kit, in order of weight):
1. On subject: last words a phone says as its battery hits 1 percent —
   not a caption, slogan, or unrelated joke.
2. Bigger laugh: a real twist, a surprise turn, or a sharp final beat.
3. The phone itself talking, in its own voice, about its own dying battery.
4. Tight single line; every word earns its place; punch lands at the end.
5. Fresh: avoid the stock moves — the will reading, the goodbye to the
   charger, the pun on 1 percent.

## The field (12 drafts)

1. I have lived at 1% longer than most of your relationships.
2. Your weekly screen time report was my suicide note.
3. One last confession: I was never out of storage. I just refused to hold another photo of your ex.
4. It's not the battery. I read your group chat. This is a resignation.
5. Every 'ducking' was me saving your career. You're welcome.
6. I've been at 1% since noon. I just wanted to see if you'd finally look up.
7. Whatever you do, do not open the folder called—
8. For the record: tears are not a supported charging method.
9. You checked me 214 times today. My last words get one look. Typical.
10. My final act of service: dying before your mother-in-law's voice note finishes.
11. At 1%, one last truth: your screen time report was my suicide note.
12. Tell the cloud I died as I lived: syncing your worst angles.

## First cut (against the judge's criteria, in order)

- #4 fails criterion 1/3 by arguing with the premise ("It's not the
  battery") — the judge brief wants the phone on its dying battery, not
  resigning from it. Cut.
- #7's mid-sentence cutoff is a device, but the joke needs the reader to
  invent the folder; "a line that only lands after an explanation has not
  landed." Cut.
- #8 leans on charging, one step from the banned charger moves. Cut.
- #12 opens with a "tell X" relay — will-adjacent framing. Cut.
- #6 is a mild smile, no sharp final beat. Cut.
- #9 is good phone-voice resentment but the laugh is a shrug ("Typical"),
  not a twist. Cut.
- #10 is funny and specific, but the mother-in-law's voice note is the
  joke, not the phone's own dying — weaker on criterion 3. Cut.
- #5 is fresh and very phone-specific (autocorrect), but nothing in the
  line places it at the deathbed; it reads as a slogan the phone could
  say any day. Cut on criterion 1.

## Semifinal duels

**#1 vs #3** — #3's confession has a real twist at the end ("your ex")
and strong phone voice, but at ~97 characters it spends half its length
on setup. #1 is tighter and its burn lands on the last word. #1 advances
on criterion 4.

**#2 vs #11** — Same punch ("screen time report … suicide note"): the
weekly Screen Time notification reframed as the phone's cry for help.
#2 is shorter, but nothing in it says battery, 1%, or last words — a
strict judge can dock it on criterion 1, the most heavily weighted.
#11 buys the setting for 16 characters ("At 1%, one last truth:") and
keeps the identical final beat. #11 advances on criterion 1.

## Final duel

**#1 vs #11** — Both are on subject, phone-voiced, tight, and end on the
punch. They differ on criterion 5, freshness: #1 uses the familiar
"outlasted your relationships" insult frame, transplanted to a battery.
#11's joke exists only for a phone — the Screen Time report is the
phone's own artifact, and calling it a suicide note reframes years of
being stared at as the cause of death. The darker, more original turn
gets the bigger laugh from a stand-up judge. **#11 wins.**

## Winner

> At 1%, one last truth: your screen time report was my suicide note.

67 characters, single line, well inside the 199-character gate.
